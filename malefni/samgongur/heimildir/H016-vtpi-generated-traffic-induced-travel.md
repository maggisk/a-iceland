## Greining

### Helstu fullyrðingar

- Skilgreining: "Generated traffic" / "induced demand" = nýjar bílferðir sem koma fram þegar vegir eru breikkaðir.
- Empíríska sönnun: 10% aukning á afkastagetu vega → 3–10% aukning á VMT (vehicle miles traveled) skammtíma, 5–15% langtíma.
- Langtíma teygjur: 0,5–1,0. Tvöföldun afkastagetu → 50–100% aukning ekinna mílna.
- Skammtíma teygjur: 0,3–0,5.
- Vélbúnaður induced demand:
  - Lengri ferðir verða ásættanlegar
  - Bílaeign vex
  - Færsla frá strætó í einkabíl
  - Breyttar staðsetningar (atvinna, búseta)
- Stefnumótandi niðurstaða: Vegabreikkun gefur minnkandi áhrif gegn umferðartöfum vegna induced demand. Forgangur ætti að vera á:
  - Almenningssamgöngu-fjárfestingar (minni rebound)
  - Eftirspurnarstýring (veggjöld, bílastæðagjöld)
  - Landnotkunarsamþætting (styttri ferðir)
  - Fjarvinna / sveigjanleg vinnutími
- Niðurstaða: sjálfbærar samgöngur þurfa samhliða stefnu — afkastageta + verðlagning + landnotkun.

## Fullt efni

### Definition
Generated traffic (also called "induced travel" or "induced demand") refers to additional vehicle trips created by road capacity expansion. When highways are widened or new routes added, travel that was previously suppressed becomes viable, partially offsetting congestion relief benefits.

### Evidence Base
The document cites elasticity research indicating that "a 10% increase in road capacity generates approximately 3-10% additional vehicle travel in the short term, and 5-15% in the long term" through mechanisms including:
- Longer trips becoming acceptable
- Increased vehicle ownership
- Shifts from transit to driving
- Changes in residential/commercial location patterns

### Key Elasticity Estimates
Long-term elasticities range from 0.5 to 1.0, meaning a doubling of road capacity produces 50-100% increase in vehicle miles traveled. Short-term effects are more modest (0.3-0.5).

### Policy Implications
The analysis argues that capacity expansion provides diminishing congestion relief due to induced demand, suggesting priorities should shift toward:
- Transit investment with less rebound effect
- Demand management (pricing, parking restrictions)
- Land-use integration reducing trip lengths
- Telecommuting and flexible work arrangements

### Recommendation
Rather than perpetual highway expansion, sustainable transportation requires combined strategies addressing supply (transit), demand (pricing), and land-use patterns simultaneously.
